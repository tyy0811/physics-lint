# v2.1 round-prose-2 N-asymmetry cross-review

## 1. Headline verdict

**Mixed.** The N=12 vs N=20 choice is substantively methodology-consistency-honoring after the D0-22 trail is read, but the application-facing cover-letter sentence initially reads like "harder rung got fewer trials because cost" and asks too much of a first-time Stuttgart/Munich reviewer.

## 2. First-impression read

The first thing my eye lands on in the cover-letter surface is the parenthetical:

> "...extending physics-lint's harness substrate-detection layer to a second LagrangeBench substrate class (open-driven-dissipative) via dam-break-2D (D0-22 + amendments 1, 2; rung-4c at N=12 per-stack vs rung-4a/4b at N=20 — D0-22 amendment 2 records the cost-driven N reduction transparently), with the same SARIF schema as TGV-2D conservation and no consumer-side accommodation needed beyond two renderer generalizations (multi-schema-version filtering and D-entry extraction; see plan v2.1 §1.2 pattern B)." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/docs/physics-lint-validation-plan-v2.1.md:89`)

Unsanitized read: this is honest, but it does not fully land. "Cost-driven N reduction" is exactly the phrase that makes a reviewer think the harder / more expensive rung got fewer trials and then received a methodology label afterward. The sentence tells me where the explanation lives, but it does not tell me the key validity-preserving fact: the rung-4c claim is structural row identity / schema behavior, not a sampling-power claim, and the structural claim is stated elsewhere to hold at N>=2.

On second reading, with D0-22 amendment 2 and the rung-4c writeup in view, the asymmetry is not a methodological gap. The project did not hide the timeout, did not compare N=12 against N=20 as if uniform, and canonicalized both dam-break stacks to the same N. The prose just leaves the cover-letter reader in the suspicious state for too long.

## 3. Specific concerns

### Concern 1 — The cover-letter sentence foregrounds cost before validity

**Trigger quote.**

> "rung-4c at N=12 per-stack vs rung-4a/4b at N=20 — D0-22 amendment 2 records the cost-driven N reduction transparently" (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/docs/physics-lint-validation-plan-v2.1.md:89`)

**Why it lands wrong.**

The reviewer interpretation chain is: dam-break is the new substrate class; new substrate is likely the harder case; harder case has fewer trajectories; the stated reason is cost; "transparently" sounds like a disclosure defense, not a methodological defense. This does not prove a gap, but it creates the gap-shaped first impression. The missing bridge is that rung-4c's load-bearing claim is not "statistical estimate at matched N"; it is "same rule schema and identical structural rows across stacks under a second substrate class."

**Suggested rewrite.**

> "...extending physics-lint's harness substrate-detection layer to a second LagrangeBench substrate class (open-driven-dissipative) via dam-break-2D, with both dam-break stacks canonicalized at N=12 after the N=20 production fire exceeded timeout (D0-22 amendment 2). Because the rung-4c claim is structural schema behavior / row identity rather than a sampling-power comparison, the N reduction is recorded as an in-rung amendment rather than hidden behind cross-rung presentational parity; rung-4a/4b remain N=20."

This is longer, but it pays for itself because the application-facing sentence must not require the reviewer to infer the validity argument from a D-entry pointer.

### Concern 2 — The strongest defense is buried in the rung writeup, not in the cover-letter surface

**Trigger quote.**

> "The structural claims hold at N≥2 and are unaffected; cross-rung N parity is the cost paid for the smoke-discovered-drift-gets-in-rung-correction discipline (§5.1)." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/docs/2026-05-07-rung-4c-substrate-class-extension-table.md:156`)

**Why it lands wrong.**

This sentence is the best defense of the asymmetry. It directly answers my objection: N parity is not load-bearing because the claim is structural. But it is not present in the cover-letter sentence. A first-time reviewer who starts at §5.3 sees "cost-driven N reduction transparently," not "structural claim unaffected." That sequencing matters. "Transparent cost reduction" is weaker than "validity-preserving in-rung correction because the measured claim does not depend on N=20 parity."

**Suggested rewrite.**

Carry this exact logic into §0 §5.3:

> "...rung-4c ships at N=12 per stack rather than rung-4a/4b's N=20 because the N=20 dam-break fire exceeded the production timeout; D0-22 amendment 2 records the correction. The rung-4c claim is structural schema behavior / row identity, which holds once both stacks are exercised at the same positive N, so cross-rung N parity is not the validity condition."

I would avoid "N>=2" in the cover letter unless the application reader already knows why `N=1` is insufficient for the cross-stack table. "Same positive N" or "both stacks exercised at matched N=12" is less brittle in application prose.

### Concern 3 — §3.1 P1 lands better than the cover letter, but "presentational uniformity" can sound self-serving

**Trigger quote.**

> "N = 12 reflects D0-22 amendment 2's cost-driven reduction (per-traj inference cost ~10× plan estimate; in-rung methodology-consistency over presentational uniformity)." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/docs/physics-lint-validation-plan-v2.1.md:55`)

**Why it lands wrong.**

This mostly works because it includes the cost multiplier and names the tradeoff. The weak part is "presentational uniformity." To a skeptical reviewer, matched N is not merely presentational unless you explain why it is not load-bearing for this specific claim. Without that explanation, the text seems to define away a real design difference as cosmetic.

**Suggested rewrite.**

> "N = 12 reflects D0-22 amendment 2's cost-driven reduction (per-traj inference cost ~10× plan estimate). The rung-4c claim is structural schema behavior / row identity at matched within-rung N, so preserving cross-rung N=20 parity would have bought presentation symmetry, not additional validity for the substrate-class-extension claim."

This keeps the author's thesis but states the premise that makes it defensible.

### Concern 4 — Considered and cleared: D0-22 amendment 2 is substantively strong

**Trigger quote.**

> "Option B ("ship at N=12 with honest amendment") fits the established pattern: smoke surfaces a drift, the rung documents the drift, the data ships at the value the budget supports, the structural claims hold at N≥2 and are unaffected." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/DECISIONS.md:2295`)

**Why it lands fine on second reading.**

This is the line that clears the methodology concern for me. It gives the causal history, the alternative considered, and the validity argument. It also makes the asymmetry symmetric within rung-4c: SEGNN's partial N=12 is not compared against GNS at N=20; GNS is deliberately run at N=12 to match the canonical rung-4c N. That is not a corner-cut if the claim really is structural.

**Suggested rewrite.**

No substantive rewrite needed in `DECISIONS.md`. If anything changes, it should be a pointer from §5.3 / §3.1 back to this exact argument, not a change to the D-entry.

### Concern 5 — Considered and cleared: the README gives a fair top-level synthesis

**Trigger quote.**

> "Both stacks (SEGNN-DAM2D + GNS-DAM2D, N=12 trajs per amendment 2's cost-driven reduction) emit byte-identical structural rows." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/README.md:45`)

**Why it lands fine on second reading.**

This sentence is compact and mostly good. It does not bury the N reduction, and it immediately states what the N=12 data is used for: byte-identical structural rows. That juxtaposition helps. The only reason the application-facing sentence underperforms is that it separates "cost-driven N reduction" from the structural-row claim.

**Suggested rewrite.**

No edit required here. If the author wants maximal consistency, "per amendment 2's cost-driven, validity-preserving reduction" would be acceptable, but it is not necessary. The README already gives enough context.

### Concern 6 — The risks register is candid and should stay candid

**Trigger quote.**

> "D0-22 amendment 2 records the in-rung methodology-consistency-over-presentational-parity choice (Option B over Option A at amendment time; see writeup §6 item 8 for the verbatim reasoning trail)." (`external_validation/_rollout_anchors/methodology/docs/physics-lint-validation-plan-v2.1.md:99`)

**Why it lands mostly fine.**

This is the right register for a risk entry: it names the production-fire failure, the bad estimate, and the forward mitigation. The one phrase I would still watch is "presentational parity" for the same reason as above. In a risk register, though, the surrounding sentence already gives the timeout mechanics and forward timeout refactor. This does not need to be the primary edit.

**Suggested rewrite.**

Optional:

> "D0-22 amendment 2 records the in-rung methodology-consistency-over-cross-rung-N-parity choice (Option B over Option A at amendment time; see writeup §6 item 8 for the verbatim reasoning trail)."

That is less rhetorically loaded than "presentational parity" and lets the D-entry make the stronger "not validity-load-bearing" argument.
